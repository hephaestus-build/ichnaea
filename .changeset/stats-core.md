First statistics core. `compareCounts` compares one binary measure per estimand. It takes the measure's value type and refuses anything but `binary`, so the items of a measure declared as a proportion are never passed as trials. For `fixed-cases`, every completed trial counts: each case with at least one completed trial per arm enters the stratified Miettinen–Nurminen score interval and test with equal case weight (the mean of per-case differences), and a case with none in an arm is excluded and reported. `compareCounts` takes no score options, so a caller cannot select a variant of that interval, such as one without the N/(N−1) factor. The regression test is the exact stratified conditional test, or a within-case permutation test when trial counts differ. For `sampled-cases`, Tango's skewness-corrected interval from 300 cases (experimental); below 300 cases, or with more than one case per family, the comparison is estimate-only. Each result names its regression method and has two results, `regression` and `nonInferiority`, each saying whether it is validated and why not. No non-inferiority result is validated yet: the validated margin floor is not set, because exact searches found coverage below the bar at margins from 0.05 to 0.185 with 5 to 8 cases and 3 trials in a first search, and at 9 and 10 cases with mixed trial counts in a second. The change that sets the floor lists each point. A regression can be shown, a non-inferiority result cannot, until the floor is set. Proportion, score and ordinal measures have no validated method yet. Also Wilson intervals (descriptive), Holm and Benjamini–Yekutieli adjustment, closed-form floors, a simulated minimum detectable effect and a seeded Mersenne Twister. The package has no runtime dependency: its Mersenne Twister and normal functions are its own, checked against numpy, R and 60-digit values. Seeded random streams are the same as before; values derived through the normal functions can differ in the last digits. Checked against ratesci and R's exact Mantel–Haenszel test, by exact enumeration, and by simulation under a two-stage rule (see VALIDATION.md).
