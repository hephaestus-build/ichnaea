# Requirements

Ichnaea's system requirements. Each one has an ID, a priority and an acceptance criterion that a test can show.

- **IDs are stable.** Code, tests and changelogs cite `REQ-nn`. A requirement that is dropped keeps its number, and a new one gets the next free number. The sections group them by area, so the numbers are not in order.
- **Priority.** **M** is the first usable release: offline paired decisions in CI on synthetic or your own data. **S** is release 1.0 (pre-change checks, the drift canary, configuration search, judges, human rating, personal data under enforced governance) or 2.0 (online monitors). **C** is later.
- **Personal data.** Personal data cannot leave through an Ichnaea command before the S-level governance exists: the `beforeEgress` hook denies every data class except `none` by default (REQ-126, M).
- **Acceptance criteria** name the test or fixture that shows the requirement. A requirement is met only when that test exists and would fail if the requirement did not hold.
- § 13 lists what Ichnaea deliberately does not do, with the reasons.

## 7. Statistics and decisions

| ID | Ichnaea shall | P | Acceptance criterion |
|---|---|---|---|
| REQ-48 | Ship the statistics as a separate pure package used by the decision, monitor, judge and report plugins. | M | The package has no runtime dependency on other Ichnaea packages. |
| REQ-49 | State the estimand of every interval (fixed cases or sampled cases) and match the uncertainty to it. | M | Each interval in a decision carries its estimand. A curated suite with one replicate produces no verdict. A plan that claims `sampled-cases` on a curated or promoted pool is refused. |
| REQ-50 | Compare configurations item by item, with effect sizes and intervals. Under `fixed-cases`, weight every case equally, use the stratified Miettinen–Nurminen score interval with the N/(N−1) factor for non-inferiority and the exact stratified conditional test for regression. Under `sampled-cases`, the methods are experimental: a decision built on one states the limitation. Wilson intervals are descriptive only and never enter a decision. For a score or proportion measure (one value in [0, 1] per trial, items of one output reduced to one value), REGRESSION uses a within-case permutation test on the equal-weight mean difference, and non-inferiority stays estimate-only until its own floor study passes. Never use Wald intervals below 300 items. Every result states whether its inputs lie inside the validated range, and outside it says so instead of claiming validation. | M | Unit tests match published values, including the decision outputs (p-values, verdict level, margin sign, test tail). Validation follows the validation rule (§ 22). The grid includes fewer than 10 cases, unequal replicate counts and missingness tied to the effect. A two-case fixture with unequal replicates gives the mean of the per-case differences. A fixture where some cases keep only one completed candidate trial gives the same estimate as the full equal-weight estimator, not a dropped-case estimate. Each comparison states two validity results. `regression.validated` is true only for binary outcomes, checked in code, at least 5 cases, at least one completed trial per arm in every case, at most 3 trials per case and arm, and replicate patterns inside the validated grid. `nonInferiority.validated` also needs an established validated margin floor, a case count inside the validated range for non-inferiority (at least 12, and at most the largest number of cases the validation covered), equal planned trial counts in both arms of every case, completed counts that differ between the arms of a case only in the one-trial pattern the validation covered, and an effective non-inferiority margin (the margin minus any safety margin) inside the validated margin range: at or above the floor that `packages/stats/VALIDATION.md` states, and at most 0.30. Until the floor is established, `nonInferiority.validated` is false and states why. REGRESSION needs `regression.validated`. NON-INFERIOR, and so PASS, needs both. A reported interval is marked not validated whenever `nonInferiority.validated` is false. With the floor not established, a fixture with a real drop gives REGRESSION, and no fixture gives PASS. The planner refuses a primary measure whose effective margin lies outside the range, a placeholder margin included. The grid includes heterogeneous effects with most cases at rate 1, at mean zero and small negative means. A test fails when any threshold of the validation rule (§ 22) is loosened. The type-I check of the validation harness runs the asymptotic stratified score test at 0, which exceeds the type-I bar, and fails it. |
| REQ-51 | Decide non-inferiority against a prespecified margin per primary measure at an unadjusted one-sided level (intersection-union). Apply Holm only to the tests that can produce REGRESSION, over the fixed family of primary measures that the plan declares. An unavailable or unvalidated test counts as unable to reject (p = 1), and the family never shrinks after missingness or a method's eligibility is seen. Support an extra safety margin per measure. | M | Truth-table tests for every verdict. A fixture where one primary is unavailable keeps the Holm family at its declared size. A safety margin shifts the threshold as specified. A simulation with four correlated primary measures and no change keeps the rate of REGRESSION verdicts at or below α. |
| REQ-52 | Report negative and positive flips with both outputs, and use only deterministic checks as hard gates. | M | A stochastic must-pass case is a primary measure, never a hard gate. |
| REQ-53 | Produce one of PASS, REGRESSION, INCONCLUSIVE, INVALID, with the coverage rules that lead to INVALID. | M | Every verdict has a test. INVALID never maps to a pass. |
| REQ-54 | Compute a power statement before the run, on each measure's applicable cases, with three outputs named apart: the best-case non-inferiority floor, the probability of PASS at no change (π₀), and the decrease the regression test detects at the target power. Keep it in the plan, and refuse an underpowered plan in the research preset. Every decision carries the plan's power statement. A decision that is not INVALID adds a run-time MDE from the base arm's per-case rates, labelled with that assumption. An INVALID decision does not compute it and states "not computed: run invalid". A run-time MDE never changes the verdict of its own run, and one from an INVALID run is never the variance assumption of a later plan. | M | The planner prints the three outputs per measure on its applicable cases (a fixture whose four measures apply to 60, 60, 52 and 17 cases prints those counts). None of them is labelled "smallest detectable drop". The research preset refuses a plan whose MDE exceeds its margin. An INVALID fixture runs no power simulation, and its decision carries the plan's statement and the reason. A power entry that was not computed states why, and is never a bare NaN or null. |
| REQ-55 | Report variation from item sampling and from non-determinism separately. | S | Reports with replicates show both components. |
| REQ-56 | Compare missingness between arms. Whenever a primary measure has a missing trial in either arm, add a sensitivity analysis that tests each verdict against the imputation that works against it: for non-inferiority, missing candidate trials fail and missing base trials pass; for regression, the reverse. A verdict that the sensitivity analysis changes becomes INCONCLUSIVE. | M | A run with missing trials shows the comparison and the sensitivity result. A fixture where 6 of 60 cases lose their failing candidate trials ends INCONCLUSIVE, not PASS. The same fixture with 12 added infrastructure failures on passing base trials, so that both arms miss 12, also ends INCONCLUSIVE. |
| REQ-57 | Compare more than two configurations with multiplicity control against a control or for selection of the best. | C | A three-arm suite gives Holm-adjusted comparisons. |
| REQ-58 | Validate the statistics package against published values and simulation studies of coverage, type-I error and false-alarm rate. Python or R oracles may run in tests only. Interval and test validation comes first. False-alarm validation of CUSUM and confidence sequences comes with the drift canary. | M | CI runs the simulations. The runtime has no Python or R dependency. Once the drift canary exists, the simulated yearly false-alarm rate of the canary is within its budget. |
| REQ-119 | Support superiority claims and comparisons of more than two systems: effect sizes with intervals for every pair against a reference, rankings with rank intervals, and the same paired estimands as the non-inferiority rule. A ranking never gets a verdict. | S | On a simulated three-system study the rank intervals meet the coverage part of the validation rule (§ 22). |
| REQ-122 | Export a frozen plan as a preregistration package (plan, decision rule, analysis code digest, power statement) in a form an external registry accepts (OSF, Zenodo), and record the external identifier in the plan. | S | The research preset refuses a confirmatory run whose plan has no external timestamp. The export validates against the registry's upload format. |

## 22. Validation rule for statistical methods

Several requirements (REQ-41, REQ-50, REQ-111, REQ-119) cite this rule. It decides whether an interval or a test is valid on a grid of simulated conditions. The nominal one-sided level is α = 0.05, so nominal coverage is 0.95.

1. **Screen.** Simulate each grid point with 6,000 repetitions in pull-request CI, or 10,000 in the nightly job. A point whose coverage estimate is at least 0.94 + 2·SE passes.
2. **Confirm** every other point. Compute it exactly where enumeration is cheap (few cases, a single proportion, the type-I error of an exact test). Otherwise re-run it with an independent seed until SE ≤ 0.0005, about 190,000 repetitions.
3. **Coverage.** A confirmed point fails if its estimate + 3·SE is below 0.94. An exactly computed point fails below 0.94.
4. **Type-I error.** The same two stages. A confirmed point fails if its estimate − 3·SE is above 0.05. An exactly computed point fails above 0.05. This bar applies to every test that can produce REGRESSION.
5. **Grid mean.** The method fails if the mean coverage over the grid + 3·SE is below 0.95.
6. **Reporting.** Pull-request CI runs the screen and the committed, seeded confirmations of known candidates. The nightly job confirms new candidates. Every run prints the thresholds in effect.

The grid includes fewer than 10 cases, unequal replicate counts, missingness tied to the effect, and heterogeneous effects where most cases pass every trial. `packages/stats/VALIDATION.md` holds the grid, the results and the validated domain, including the validated margin floor.
