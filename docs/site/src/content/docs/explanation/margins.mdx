A margin is the largest decrease in a measure that you accept.
For example, a margin of 15 points means: a candidate may answer up to 15 points worse than the base and still pass.

The margin is a decision about your product, not about statistics.
The owner of the measure sets it.

## Register it before you compare

You write the margin in the suite, with a reason, before any comparison uses it.
The support example registers its margin like this:

```json
{
  "measure": "support/facts-stated",
  "margin": 0.15,
  "marginStatus": "registered",
  "marginReason": "Twelve cases cannot see smaller drops; the floor at three replicates is about 0.08."
}
```

A margin that you choose after you see the results can always be made to pass.
That is why a margin with the status `placeholder` gives no verdict. Ichnaea runs the plan as an estimate only.

## The floor: the smallest margin your suite can clear

Even when nothing changes and every trial passes, your suite has a limit.
With K cases and r trials per case, the smallest margin that can pass is about

> floor = z² / (K(r − ½) + z²), with z = 1.645

| Cases | Floor with 2 trials | Floor with 3 trials |
| ----- | ------------------- | ------------------- |
| 17    | 9.6 points          | 6.0 points          |
| 20    | 8.3 points          | 5.1 points          |
| 60    | 2.9 points          | 1.8 points          |
| 250   | 0.7 points          | 0.4 points          |

If your margin is below the floor, your suite can never pass, even when nothing changes.
The planner checks this before the first trial and says so.

## Why no candidate can pass yet

A non-inferiority result is validated only for margins from a smallest validated margin up to 30 points.
That smallest margin is not set yet.
The studies found case structures in which the lower bound is too optimistic.
So until that smallest margin is set, Ichnaea gives no PASS. An unchanged candidate gets INCONCLUSIVE.
A real decrease beyond run-to-run noise still gives REGRESSION.

## Limits

- A margin of 0 is not a non-inferiority test.
- A claim that a candidate is better than the base is never validated.
